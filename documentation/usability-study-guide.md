# Closet Circle Usability Study Guide

Use this guide for a small moderated study with three to five participants. The goal is to observe behavior, not to persuade participants that the interface works. Do not record names, email addresses, or other identifying details in the repository.

## Research questions

1. Can a new seller create and publish a rental listing without help?
2. Can a buyer discover a rentable item, choose dates, submit a request, and find its status?
3. Which labels, states, or transitions cause hesitation or errors?

## Participant introduction

> Closet Circle is a prototype marketplace for buying and renting clothing from other people. I am testing the product, not you. Please think aloud as you work. I may ask what you expect to happen, but I will not guide you unless you become completely blocked. This session should take about 15 minutes. I will record task outcomes and anonymous notes only. You can stop at any time.

Confirm that the participant agrees before beginning.

## Task 1: Publish a rental listing

Give the participant a sample item and image, then say:

> You want to make this item available for rent. Starting from your account, create the listing and make it visible in the marketplace.

Record:

- Whether the participant published successfully without assistance.
- Time from task start to publication.
- First click and navigation path.
- Hesitations, errors, backtracking, and questions.
- Whether draft, preview, rental availability, and publish status were understood.

## Task 2: Request a rental and find its status

Say:

> Find an item you would rent, request it for a three-day period next month, and show me where you would return later to check whether the seller approved it.

Record:

- Whether the participant completed the request without assistance.
- Time from task start to submitted request.
- Whether estimated price and dates were understood.
- Whether the participant found the rental dashboard and correctly interpreted the status.
- Hesitations, errors, backtracking, and questions.

## Follow-up questions

- What did you expect to happen at the point where you hesitated most?
- Which part felt easiest?
- Which part felt least trustworthy or clear?
- What information did you want but could not find?
- If you could change one thing, what would it be?

## Prioritization

After all sessions, group repeated observations. Prioritize the problem that combines high frequency with meaningful task impact. Document the decision with this structure:

- **Observed problem:** What participants actually did or said.
- **Evidence:** How many participants encountered it and on which task.
- **Decision:** The smallest product or interface change selected.
- **Tradeoff:** What was deliberately left unchanged and why.
- **Success measure:** The behavior expected to improve in a second round.

## Before-and-after comparison

Run the same affected task with a fresh set of participants or with the original participants after enough time has passed to reduce recall. Compare completion without help, task time, errors, and hesitation points. Report the raw sample size and observed values; do not generalize a small study into broad market claims.
